# Emotion Engine

Methods draft based on the current implementation · September 14, 2026

## State Representation and Module Design

We implement the emotion engine as a separate state-transition module within the emergency department world model. Its task is to predict how a received utterance changes the listener's emotional state. The predicted state is subsequently supplied to the patient or family-member dialogue generator. This separates emotion updating from utterance generation and provides an explicit state that can persist across interactions and be evaluated independently of the generated dialogue.

The patient and family member each have a four-component emotion vector comprising fear, sadness, overwhelm, and shame. Fear represents perceived threats to health, safety, or survival; sadness represents experienced or anticipated loss and helplessness; overwhelm represents informational or emotional demands exceeding the listener's processing capacity; and shame represents blame, judgment, exposure, humiliation, or threats to dignity. These dimensions form an operational representation for the simulation, rather than an exhaustive taxonomy of human emotion. Multiple emotions can coexist.

Each component takes one of three ordered values: absent, present, or extreme. Absent indicates that the emotion is not present; present covers non-crisis emotional activation; and extreme denotes a crisis-level response that compromises continued participation. The default initialization sets all four components to absent without a model call. This establishes a common simulation baseline rather than estimating a real patient's emotional state on arrival. Clinicians and nurses do not maintain corresponding emotion vectors in the current implementation.

## Input Construction and Update Eligibility

The runtime adapter receives the current emotion state, newly committed messages, recent messages, and role-profile information. Each message identifies its speaker, recipient, and content. A message triggers an update only when it is explicitly addressed to the listener and was produced by another role. Self-utterances, empty messages, and messages addressed elsewhere are excluded from direct updates. The turn loop additionally determines whether the patient can perceive the conversation and whether the family member is considered present. Patient emotion updates are generally suspended during unconsciousness or sedation, while utterances heard before sedation within the same turn are retained for processing. In the current simulation, children under three are not treated as patient emotion-dialogue agents; communication is instead modeled through the participating family member.

For each eligible utterance, the engine receives the listener's current four-dimensional state, listener and speaker identities, the utterance, textual clinical context, and a dialogue context string. The adapter constructs clinical context from the scenario narrative and available persona or trait information. The model therefore receives clinical information expressed in text, although the update function does not directly accept a live vital-sign vector. The initialization interface retains legacy vitals and traits arguments, but these do not affect the default all-absent initialization.

Within an adapter call, eligible utterances are processed sequentially, and the updated state from one utterance becomes the input state for the next. The dialogue context initially contains up to eight messages from the supplied recent-message list, formatted as speaker and content, and is extended as eligible utterances are processed without reapplying that limit within the batch. The supplied list may already include messages from the current turn; the current implementation therefore does not guarantee that this context contains only information preceding the utterance under evaluation. State history is maintained in the returned trajectory object, but the complete sequence of historical emotion vectors is not automatically included in the prompt.

## Prompt Construction

The transition model uses a shared prompt template containing six elements: the four emotion definitions; listener and speaker identities; current emotion levels; clinical and dialogue context; transition rules; and the required response structure. The target utterance is placed at the end of the prompt. Emotion definitions are maintained in an editable text block and inserted through an explicit template variable when the prompt is constructed. Editing this block changes the definitions presented to the model; adding a tracked emotion still requires changes to the state schema.

The transition instructions encode persistence, bounded movement, and crisis handling. Emotions should retain their current level unless the utterance provides a concrete reason for change, and an unaddressed emotion should not disappear merely because it is not mentioned. The prompt distinguishes acknowledgment from resolution: empathy alone does not justify removing an emotion, and a return to absent requires resolution of its trigger under the stated level semantics. Serious newly disclosed threats are instructed to raise fear to at least present, even when communicated calmly, while improvement and resolution are distinguished from deterioration by their meaning rather than by isolated clinical terms. Overwhelm, sadness, and shame are assessed using their respective definitions.

Changes are generally instructed to remain within one ordinal level per utterance. A direct absent-to-extreme transition is permitted only for exceptional utterances meeting the crisis criterion. Extreme refers to predicted breakdown in the listener's ability to continue participating, rather than the severity of the medical information alone. These semantic constraints are supplied as model instructions; they are not all enforced through deterministic validation.

## Prompt-Strategy Variants

Three prompt variants are implemented using the same state representation, definitions, transition rules, input fields, and output contract. The default zero-shot variant contains the shared instructions without labeled input-output demonstrations. The few-shot variant appends four fixed demonstrations, following the use of examples supplied in context without task-specific parameter updates described by [Brown et al. (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165). The current demonstrations illustrate unchanged emotions during a routine procedure, increases in fear and overwhelm after urgent risk disclosure, decreases after an explicitly improving situation, and increased shame after blame. Each demonstration includes an input, a complete output vector, and a short explanation. They do not currently demonstrate a sadness transition or an extreme output.

The third variant, named `react` in the implementation, adds an internal observe-reason-act-check sequence. It asks the model to identify relevant evidence, assess each emotion, select valid levels, and review the result against the transition rules before returning JSON. This is a single-call structured-review prompt. It does not execute external actions or acquire observations from tools, and is therefore distinguished from the interleaved reasoning-and-acting framework introduced by [Yao et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629). All variants use the same final response fields, including a brief explanation; the comparison concerns the additional demonstrations or review instructions, rather than the presence versus absence of any requested explanation. The engine itself performs no task-specific training or parameter updates.

## Response Processing and State Persistence

The model is asked to return a JSON object containing `new_state`, `evidence`, and `reasoning`. The new-state object contains the four emotion levels; evidence identifies the relevant utterance fragment or indicates the absence of a concrete trigger; and reasoning explains changes relative to the previous state. A coercion layer accommodates some response-format variations, such as explanation fields nested within the state object, before validating the emotion levels against the permitted categories. In the current implementation, omitted emotion components receive the state model's absent defaults rather than necessarily causing rejection.

An explicit post-processing rule enforces the absorbing behavior of extreme: any component already at extreme is kept at extreme even if the model proposes a lower level. The remaining semantic rules, including persistence and the exceptional threshold for a two-level increase, rely on the prompt. The engine returns the updated state, an appended trajectory history, and the most recent evidence and explanation. If an exception occurs during a batch of utterances, the runtime adapter records the error, stops processing the remainder of that batch, and returns the last successfully obtained local state.

The adapter converts the result into `AgentEmotion`, with explanations collected in an optional notes field. `StateManager` is responsible for writing patient and family-member states into `GlobalState.psych_state` and recording emotion-update events. The runtime state does not separately persist every utterance's evidence or the complete intermediate trajectory; the standalone evaluation path stores evidence and reasoning in its prediction records. These explanations support inspection but are not treated as independently verified psychological observations.

## Integration with the World Model

The observation builder supplies the updated patient emotion state to the patient generator and the relative emotion state to the family-member generator. These states condition subsequent role responses while clinical facts remain subject to the world model's information boundaries. Emotion updates do not regenerate previously committed dialogue or directly select the next speaker. They also do not directly modify vital signs or treatment effects: the physiology adapter has a separate input path without a dedicated emotion-state input. Downstream effects may instead arise through later dialogue, subsequent clinical-agent decisions, and termination.

In the current termination logic, an extreme patient emotion can end the encounter, whereas an extreme family-member emotion does not independently trigger the same termination condition. The adapter can still process later utterances in its current batch before the turn loop checks for termination. When no active emotion engine is supplied, `NoopEmotionEngine` preserves the existing emotion state. This provides an explicit alternative to LLM-based updating, although dialogue generators may still express emotions from other contextual information. The module consequently supports evaluation of explicit emotional state tracking within the simulation without itself establishing clinical or educational validity.
